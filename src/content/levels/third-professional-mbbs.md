title: Third Professional MBBS
description: >
  The third professional examination covers Forensic Medicine and Community
  Medicine, alongside the transition into clinical clerkships.
kind: exam
introduction: >
  The Third Professional MBBS examination covers Forensic Medicine and
  Community Medicine at most Pakistani universities. It usually falls in the
  third or fourth year, overlapping with the start of clinical rotations —
  which is fitting, because both subjects are applied rather than
  laboratory-based: one deals with the legal duties of a doctor, the other
  with the health of populations.


  This site groups the subjects the way most universities following the
  UHS-style pattern examine them, but the grouping varies noticeably between
  institutions — some examine Forensic Medicine with the final professional,
  and some split Community Medicine across two parts. Always confirm the
  structure that applies to your own batch.
faqs:
  - question: Which subjects are in the Third Professional MBBS?
    answer: >
      Forensic Medicine and Community Medicine at most universities, sometimes
      with subjects such as ENT and Ophthalmology examined alongside or in a
      neighbouring part. The exact combination varies, so check your
      university's regulations.
  - question: How is the exam structured at most universities?
    answer: >
      Usually a written paper of MCQs and SEQs plus a practical and viva for
      each subject. Forensic Medicine vivas often centre on specimen and
      injury identification, while Community Medicine papers include data
      interpretation and biostatistics calculations.
  - question: Why are these subjects examined together?
    answer: >
      Both are taught in the clinical years and both concern the doctor's
      duties beyond the individual patient — legal duties in Forensic
      Medicine, and duties to the community's health in Community Medicine.
      The pairing is a convention of most Pakistani curricula rather than a
      universal rule.
  - question: How much do these subjects matter for future practice?
    answer: >
      More than students often expect. Forensic Medicine governs how you
      handle medico-legal cases, certificates, and court appearances, while
      Community Medicine underpins public health posts, screening programmes,
      and the preventive side of everyday practice.
