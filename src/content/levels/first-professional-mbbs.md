introduction: >
  The First Professional MBBS examination is the first major hurdle of medical
  school. It covers the three pre-clinical subjects — Anatomy, Physiology, and
  Biochemistry — usually examined at the end of the first one to two years,
  depending on whether your university runs an annual or modular system. Most
  Pakistani universities following the UHS-style pattern examine all three
  subjects together in a single professional exam.


  This site groups the material the way most Pakistani and PMDC-pattern
  universities examine it, but the exact grouping, sequencing, and internal
  marks split vary by university. Always check your own university's
  curriculum and past papers for the specific format that applies to you.
faqs:
  - question: Which subjects are in the First Professional MBBS?
    answer: >
      Anatomy, Physiology, and Biochemistry. Some universities also include a
      short Behavioural Sciences or Islamic Studies component in the internal
      assessment, but the three pre-clinical subjects carry the professional
      examination weight.
  - question: How is the exam structured at most universities?
    answer: >
      Most universities use a combination of multiple-choice questions (MCQs),
      short essay questions (SEQs), and practical or viva voce (OSPE/OSCE)
      components for each subject. The exact split of written, practical, and
      viva marks varies, so check your university's most recent regulations
      rather than relying on seniors' recollections.
  - question: How much time does the First Professional usually take?
    answer: >
      At most universities the first professional material is taught over one
      to one and a half years in an annual system, or spread across the first
      two semesters in a modular system. Anatomy generally consumes the
      largest share of study time.
  - question: Do I need to pass all three subjects at once?
    answer: >
      It depends on your university's rules. Many allow you to clear a failed
      subject in a supplementary or carry-over attempt while progressing, while
      some require clearing all subjects together. Confirm with your own
      institution, because these policies change and are applied per
      regulations in force for your batch.
