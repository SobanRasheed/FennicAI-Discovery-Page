  The second professional examination covers Pathology, Pharmacology, and
  Microbiology — the bridge between pre-clinical science and clinical medicine.
kind: exam
introduction: >
  The Second Professional MBBS examination covers the three para-clinical
  subjects: Pathology, Pharmacology, and Microbiology. It is usually taken
  after the second or third year of the degree, once the pre-clinical
  foundation is complete. These subjects are where basic science turns into
  disease mechanisms, drugs, and infectious agents — the direct vocabulary of
  the wards.


  This site groups the material the way most Pakistani universities following
  the UHS-style pattern examine it, but the exact grouping and timing vary by
  university, and some institutions split these subjects differently between
  professional parts. Check your own university's curriculum and past papers
  for the format that applies to your batch.
faqs:
  - question: Which subjects are in the Second Professional MBBS?
    answer: >
      Pathology, Pharmacology, and Microbiology at most universities. Some
      curricula also teach Community Medicine and Forensic Medicine during
      the same years, but they are usually examined in a later professional
      part.
  - question: How does the Second Professional differ from the First?
    answer: >
      The first professional tests normal structure and function. The second
      tests what goes wrong: disease processes in Pathology, drugs that modify
      them in Pharmacology, and the organisms that cause them in Microbiology.
      Questions lean more on mechanisms and clinical scenarios than on pure
      recall.
  - question: How is the exam structured at most universities?
    answer: >
      Typically a written paper of MCQs and SEQs plus a practical and viva
      component for each subject. Pharmacology and Microbiology papers often
      include interpretation-style questions — a clinical vignette, a drug
      name, or a lab finding — rather than isolated facts.
  - question: Is the Second Professional harder than the First?
    answer: >
      Most students find the volume more manageable than Anatomy but the
      integration harder, because each subject assumes fluency in the
      pre-clinical sciences. A weak First Professional foundation in
      Physiology and Biochemistry shows up quickly in Pharmacology.
