  working out which system broke first and how hard the other one is
  fighting back.

  - question: What is the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation?
    answer: >
      pH = 6.1 + log of bicarbonate divided by (0.03 x PaCO2). It is the
      equation behind every blood gas: the metabolic component is
      bicarbonate, which the kidneys control, and the respiratory
      component is PaCO2, which ventilation controls.
  - question: How can I tell if compensation is adequate?
    answer: >
      Use the expected compensation formulas. In metabolic acidosis,
      Winter's formula predicts PaCO2 as 1.5 x bicarbonate + 8, within a
      range of 2. If the measured PaCO2 is far from that predicted value,
      a second acid-base disorder is hiding in the same gas report.
  - question: Why does the body tolerate only such a narrow pH range?
    answer: >
      Enzymes and ion channels are proteins whose shape depends on
      electrical charge, and charge depends on pH. Outside roughly 7.35
      to 7.45, enzyme kinetics, potassium distribution across membranes,
      and cardiac conduction all begin to fail.

## What it is

Acid-base balance is the machinery that holds arterial pH between 7.35
and 7.45 despite the body generating acid continuously. Hydrated carbon
dioxide is the largest daily acid load, and metabolism adds fixed acids:
lactic acid, ketoacids, and sulfuric and phosphoric acid from protein
breakdown. Defense happens in three tiers with different clocks. Chemical
buffers, chiefly the bicarbonate system, act in seconds. Respiratory
control of PaCO2 adjusts within minutes. Renal excretion of hydrogen ions,
plus reabsorption and regeneration of bicarbonate, takes hours to days.
A disturbance of bicarbonate is metabolic, a disturbance of PaCO2 is
respiratory, and each provokes compensation from the other side, which is
exactly what a blood gas asks you to unpick.

## Why it spans subjects

In physiology, ventilation sets PaCO2 while the kidney sets bicarbonate,
so acid-base is where the respiratory and renal systems meet. In
biochemistry, the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation is buffer chemistry
applied to a living compartment, and tissue hypoxia diverts pyruvate
toward lactate, which is the anion-gap acidosis you can trace through the
[glycolysis note](/notes/glycolysis/). In pathology, ischemia and shock
injure cells partly by overwhelming these defenses: as the
[cell injury note](/notes/cell-injury/) explains, falling pH impairs
membrane pumps and enzyme function well before a cell actually dies, and
the same logic links this hub to
[inflammation and repair](/topics/inflammation-and-repair/). Interpreting
the numbers themselves is a measurement problem, which is where
[diagnostic test accuracy](/topics/diagnostic-test-accuracy/) takes over.

## Where it's examined

First Professional MBBS examines buffers, the equation, and compensation
in both physiology (blood gas reading, renal regulation) and biochemistry
(pH, buffers, anion gap), and examiners expect you to interpret a gas
report cold. Second Professional pathology revisits the topic in shock,
infarction, and renal failure. Calculation-heavy practice is the fastest
route to fluency: drill the [physiology quiz](/practice/quiz/physiology/)
and the [biochemistry quiz](/practice/quiz/biochemistry/) until the
formulas are automatic.
