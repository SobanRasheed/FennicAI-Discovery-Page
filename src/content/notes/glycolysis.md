## Introduction

Glycolysis is the first pathway every biochemistry course teaches, and the
one every examiner returns to. It is the only energy pathway that works in
every cell of the body — including red blood cells, which have no
mitochondria — because it runs in the cytosol and needs no oxygen. The
pathway splits neatly into two halves that make memorization manageable: an
**investment phase** that spends ATP to trap and split glucose, and a
**payoff phase** that harvests ATP from the two three-carbon fragments.

## The Investment Phase (Steps 1–5)

One glucose is phosphorylated, isomerized, phosphorylated again, and split
into two three-carbon sugars:

1. **Hexokinase / glucokinase** — glucose to glucose-6-phosphate; **ATP is
   consumed**. Hexokinase is everywhere with low Km and product inhibition by
   glucose-6-phosphate; glucokinase is liver and pancreatic beta-cell
   specific with high Km and no product inhibition.
2. **Phosphoglucose isomerase** — glucose-6-phosphate to
   fructose-6-phosphate.
3. **Phosphofructokinase-1 (PFK-1)** — fructose-6-phosphate to
   fructose-1,6-bisphosphate; **ATP consumed**, **rate-limiting step**.
4. **Aldolase** — cleaves fructose-1,6-bisphosphate into
   dihydroxyacetone phosphate and glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate.
5. **Triose phosphate isomerase** — converts dihydroxyacetone phosphate to
   glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate, so both halves feed forward.

## The Payoff Phase (Steps 6–10)

Each glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (two per glucose) now pays back:

6. **Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase** — oxidation and
   phosphorylation to 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate, reducing **NAD+ to NADH**.
   This is the only oxidation step of glycolysis.
7. **Phosphoglycerate kinase** — **ATP produced** by substrate-level
   phosphorylation.
8. **Phosphoglycerate mutase** — 3-phosphoglycerate to 2-phosphoglycerate.
9. **Enolase** — dehydration to phosphoenolpyruvate. *Fluoride inhibits
   enolase — the reason glucose samples go into fluoride tubes.*
10. **Pyruvate kinase** — **ATP produced**; substrate-level phosphorylation
    yielding pyruvate and ending the pathway.

| Step | Enzyme | Cofactor / energy | Regulated? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hexokinase / glucokinase | ATP → ADP | Yes — inhibited by G6P (hexokinase) |
| 3 | PFK-1 | ATP → ADP | Yes — rate-limiting; AMP, F-2,6-BP activate; ATP, citrate inhibit |
| 6 | GAPDH | NAD+ → NADH | No |
| 7 | Phosphoglycerate kinase | ATP produced | No |
| 10 | Pyruvate kinase | ATP produced | Yes — activated by F-1,6-BP; inhibited by ATP and alanine |

## Energetics

Per molecule of glucose (which yields two three-carbon units):

- **Invested:** 2 ATP (steps 1 and 3).
- **Produced:** 4 ATP (steps 7 and 10, running twice each).
- **Net anaerobically: 2 ATP**, plus 2 NADH that are consumed in reducing
  pyruvate to lactate.
- **Aerobically:** the 2 NADH enter the mitochondria through shuttles —
  the malate-aspartate shuttle yields about 2.5 ATP each, the
  glycerol-3-phosphate shuttle about 1.5 each — giving roughly **7 ATP
  equivalents from glycolysis alone** before the TCA cycle and oxidative
  phosphorylation add their share.

### Worked exam framing

An MCQ asks: *a patient in cardiogenic shock has tissue hypoxia; what is the
net ATP per glucose from glycolysis, and what accumulates?* Answer: net
2 ATP, and lactate accumulates because NADH cannot be oxidized by the
electron transport chain, so LDH regenerates NAD+ at pyruvate's expense —
the biochemistry of lactic acidosis (Type A, due to tissue hypoxia).

## Fates of Pyruvate

1. **Oxidative decarboxylation to acetyl-CoA** by the pyruvate
   dehydrogenase complex — aerobic, mitochondrial, irreversible; the link to
   the TCA cycle.
2. **Reduction to lactate** by LDH — anaerobic muscle, red cells, and the
   Cori cycle with the liver.
3. **Carboxylation to oxaloacetate** by pyruvate carboxylase — the first
   committed step of gluconeogenesis and an anaplerotic refill for the TCA
   cycle.
4. **Transamination to alanine** — the glucose-alanine cycle between muscle
   and liver.
5. In yeast, decarboxylation to acetaldehyde and ethanol — fermentation,
   tested as general biochemistry rather than human.

## Regulation: Fed versus Fasting State

The three irreversible enzymes let the pathway respond to the body's fuel
state:

- **Hexokinase vs glucokinase** — hexokinase's low Km means it works at all
  times in all tissues; the liver's glucokinase, with its high Km and its
  induction by insulin, only engages when blood glucose is high after a meal.
  This is why the liver buffers blood glucose but does not compete for it
  when glucose is scarce.
- **PFK-1 and fructose-2,6-bisphosphate** — in the fed state, insulin raises
  fructose-2,6-bisphosphate (through activation of the kinase domain of the
  bifunctional PFK-2/FBPase-2 enzyme) and glycolysis accelerates. In
  fasting, glucagon shifts the same bifunctional enzyme to its phosphatase
  mode, fructose-2,6-bisphosphate falls, PFK-1 slows, and the liver switches
  to gluconeogenesis.
- **Pyruvate kinase** — the liver isoenzyme is phosphorylated and
  inactivated by the glucagon-driven cAMP cascade, reinforcing the
  fasting-state shutdown; the muscle and brain isoenzymes are not
  glucagon-sensitive and keep glycolysis running for their own needs.

The unifying idea for the viva: **glycolysis is promoted by insulin and
inhibited by glucagon in the liver, but is always allowed to run in the
brain and red cells, which cannot burn anything else.**

## Clinical Correlations

- **LDH isoenzymes:** LDH is a tetramer of H and M subunits — LDH-1 (H4)
  is dominant in heart, LDH-5 (M4) in liver and skeletal muscle. Historically
  the LDH-1 greater than LDH-2 "flipped pattern" supported myocardial
  infarction; today troponin has replaced it, but the isoenzyme logic still
  appears in exams.
- **Lactic acidosis:** Type A from tissue hypoxia (shock, severe asthma,
  seizures); Type B without hypoxia — metformin in renal failure, alcohol,
  mitochondrial disorders. Anion-gap metabolic acidosis with elevated lactate.
- **Pyruvate kinase deficiency:** chronic haemolytic anaemia; red cells
  cannot sustain ATP and deformability. Interestingly the blocked distal
  pathway shunts intermediates to 2,3-BPG, which raises it and shifts the
  oxygen dissociation curve right, easing tissue oxygen delivery.
- **Cancer metabolism — the Warburg effect:** tumours run glycolysis to
  lactate even in oxygen, the principle behind PET imaging with
  fluorodeoxyglucose, which is trapped intracellularly once phosphorylated by
  hexokinase.
