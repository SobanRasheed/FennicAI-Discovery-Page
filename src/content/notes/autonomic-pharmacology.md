  - term: Sympathomimetic
    definition: >
      A drug that mimics sympathetic activity, either directly at receptors
      or indirectly by releasing norepinephrine.
  - term: Sympatholytic
    definition: >
      A drug that blocks sympathetic activity, most importantly alpha and
      beta receptor antagonists.
  - term: Cholinomimetic
    definition: >
      A drug that mimics parasympathetic activity, either at muscarinic
      receptors or by inhibiting acetylcholinesterase.
  - term: Anticholinesterase
    definition: >
      A drug that inhibits acetylcholinesterase, raising acetylcholine at all

      (organophosphates).
  - term: Anticholinergic
    definition: >
      A drug that blocks muscarinic receptors — atropine and its

      and tachycardia.
  - term: Catecholamine

      A transmitter with the catechol nucleus — norepinephrine,

  - term: Mydriasis-cycloplegia
    definition: >

faqs:
  - question: Why is epinephrine the drug of choice in anaphylaxis?
    answer: >
      It acts at both alpha and beta receptors at once: alpha-1
      vasoconstriction restores blood pressure and reduces angioedema,
      beta-2 relaxes bronchi, and beta-1 supports cardiac output. It is given
      intramuscularly into the anterolateral thigh; no other single drug
      covers all the life-threatening features.
  - question: Why must alpha blockade precede beta blockade in pheochromocytoma?
    answer: >
      Beta blockers remove beta-2 mediated vasodilation in skeletal muscle
      while leaving the tumor's massive catecholamine output acting on
      alpha-1 receptors — unopposed vasoconstriction can trigger a
      hypertensive crisis or pulmonary edema. Phenoxybenzamine is started
      first, and only then is a beta blocker added.
  - question: Why is neostigmine preferred to physostigmine in myasthenia gravis?
    answer: >
      Neostigmine is a quaternary amine that does not cross the blood-brain
      barrier, so it amplifies acetylcholine at the neuromuscular junction
      without central effects. Pyridostigmine, longer-acting, is the usual
      maintenance drug; physostigmine crosses into the CNS and is reserved
      for central anticholinergic poisoning.
  - question: What are the cardinal features of organophosphate poisoning?
    answer: >
      The cholinergic toxidrome of DUMBBELLS — diarrhea, urination,
      miosis, bronchorrhea and bronchospasm, bradycardia, excitation,
      lacrimation, lethargy, salivation and sweating. Treat with atropine
      for the muscarinic effects plus pralidoxime to reactivate the enzyme,
      which must be given before the phosphate-enzyme bond ages.

## Introduction

Autonomic pharmacology looks like a wall of drug names, but the whole
subject hangs on one receptor table. Learn what each receptor does to each
organ, and every drug in the class becomes predictable: an agonist at that
receptor reproduces the effect, a blocker removes it. The [pain pathways
topic hub](/topics/pain-pathways/) covers where these autonomic fibres run
and why sympathetic blocks relieve some pain syndromes.

## The Receptor Table

| Receptor | Location | Effect | Transduction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alpha-1 | Vascular smooth muscle, internal sphincters, radial muscle of iris | Vasoconstriction, sphincter contraction, mydriasis | Gq — IP3, calcium |
| Alpha-2 | Presynaptic terminals, CNS | Inhibits norepinephrine release; lowers sympathetic outflow | Gi — decreased cAMP |
| Beta-1 | Heart, kidney (juxtaglomerular cells) | Increased rate, force and conduction; renin release | Gs — increased cAMP |
| Beta-2 | Bronchi, skeletal muscle vessels, uterus, liver | Bronchodilation, vasodilation, uterine relaxation, glycogenolysis | Gs — increased cAMP |
| Beta-3 | Adipose tissue, bladder | Lipolysis; bladder relaxation | Gs |
| Nicotinic (NN) | Autonomic ganglia, adrenal medulla | Ganglionic transmission, catecholamine release | Ion channel |
| Nicotinic (NM) | Neuromuscular junction | Skeletal muscle contraction | Ion channel |
| Muscarinic M2 | Heart | Bradycardia, decreased conduction | Gi |
| Muscarinic M3 | Smooth muscle, glands, eye | Contraction of gut and bladder detrusor, secretions, miosis and accommodation | Gq |

The single most examinable pattern: **sympathetic stimulation speeds the
heart (beta-1) and constricts skin and splanchnic vessels (alpha-1) but
dilates skeletal muscle vessels (beta-2)**; **parasympathetic stimulation
slows the heart (M2) and constricts bronchi and gut glands (M3)**.

## Sympathomimetics

- **Direct-acting, catecholamines:**
  - *Epinephrine* — all receptors; anaphylaxis, resuscitation, prolongs
    local anesthesia.
  - *Norepinephrine* — alpha-1 and beta-1 (negligible beta-2): a
    vasoconstricting pressor agent in septic shock.
  - *Dopamine* — dose-dependent: renal and splanchnic vasodilation at low
    dose (D1), beta-1 then alpha-1 as the dose rises.
  - *Isoproterenol* — pure beta; historical teaching tool.
  - *Dobutamine* — beta-1 selective inotrope for acute heart failure.
- **Direct-acting, non-catecholamines:** *phenylephrine* (pure alpha-1 —
  nasal decongestant, mydriatic without cycloplegia), *albuterol/salbutamol*
  (beta-2 — asthma), *clonidine* (alpha-2 — antihypertensive, also used as
  an adjunct in pain management).
- **Indirect-acting:** *amphetamine* — displaces norepinephrine from
  terminals; also releases dopamine and serotonin (ADHD, narcolepsy).
- **Mixed:** *ephedrine* — direct plus indirect, orally active, crosses
  into the CNS; used for hypotension in obstetric spinal anesthesia.

## Sympatholytics

- **Alpha-1 blockers:** *phenoxybenzamine* (irreversible —
  pheochromocytoma), *phentolamine* (reversible), *prazosin* and
  *tamsulosin* (uroselective — benign prostatic hyperplasia). Adverse
  effect: first-dose orthostatic hypotension.
- **Alpha-2 blockers:** rarely therapeutic; phentolamine has some effect.
- **Beta blockers:** *propranolol* (non-selective — hypertension,
  arrhythmias, migraine prophylaxis, thyrotoxicosis), *metoprolol* and
  *atenolol* (beta-1 selective — safer in asthma), *carvedilol* and
  *labetalol* (mixed alpha-beta). Core adverse effects: bradycardia,
  bronchospasm, fatigue, masking of hypoglycemia.
- *Metyrosine* (blocks catecholamine synthesis) completes the class at
  textbook level.

## Parasympathomimetics

- **Choline esters:** *bethanechol* — M3 selective; urinary retention and
  gastrointestinal atony without cardiovascular effect. *Methacholine* —
  bronchial provocation testing in asthma diagnosis.
- **Natural alkaloids:** *pilocarpine* — the classic drug for open-angle
  glaucoma (contracts ciliary muscle, improves outflow) and for xerostomia.
  *Muscarine* — the mushroom poison, a teaching toxidrome.
- **Anticholinesterases (indirect):**
  - *Reversible:* edrophonium (ultra-short, diagnostic in myasthenia),
    neostigmine and pyridostigmine (myasthenia gravis; neostigmine also
    reverses non-depolarizing neuromuscular blockade), physostigmine
    (crosses the blood-brain barrier — central anticholinergic syndrome),
    donepezil (CNS — Alzheimer disease).
  - *Irreversible:* organophosphates — echothiophate as an antiquated
    glaucoma drug, and the insecticides and nerve agents of poisoning
    cases.

## Anticholinergics

- *Atropine* — bradycardia, pre-anesthetic secretion control, organophosphate
  antidote; ophthalmic use has largely given way to tropicamide.
- *Scopolamine* — motion sickness, transdermal.
- *Ipratropium* and *tiotropium* — inhaled, bronchodilator in COPD without
  systemic effect.
- *Oxybutynin, tolterodine, solifenacin* — overactive bladder.
- *Glycopyrrolate* — perioperative and with neostigmine, quaternary and
  CNS-sparing.
- *Tropicamide, cyclopentolate* — mydriasis and cycloplegia for fundoscopy.

**Anticholinergic toxidrome:** dry as a bone (anhidrosis), red as a beet
(flushing), hot as a hare (hyperthermia), blind as a bat (mydriasis with
cycloplegia), mad as a hatter (delirium), plus urinary retention and
tachycardia — and the bowel is silent.

## Clinical Pearls and Worked Framing

A viva favourite runs like this: *a patient on timolol eye drops for
glaucoma develops wheeze — explain.* Timolol is a non-selective beta blocker
absorbed systemically from the conjunctiva; beta-2 blockade in the bronchi
precipitates asthma. The fix is switching to a prostaglandin analogue or
pilocarpine. This single vignette tests receptor selectivity, route of
administration, and end-organ prediction at once — the entire subject in one
question.

Then the classics: anaphylaxis gets intramuscular **epinephrine**, never an
antihistamine first; myasthenia gets **pyridostigmine** with edrophonium or
the ice-pack test to diagnose; organophosphate exposure gets **atropine plus
pralidoxime**; and clonidine, the alpha-2 agonist, shows up twice — as an
antihypertensive that must never be stopped abruptly, and as an epidural or
systemic adjunct in chronic pain, where it dampens sympathetic traffic.
