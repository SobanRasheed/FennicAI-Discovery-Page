---
question: >
  In a stable population of 5,000 people, 25 new cases of a chronic
  disease are diagnosed each year and the average duration of the
  disease after diagnosis is 4 years. Assuming a steady state,
  approximately how many people in this population have the disease at
  any given time?
type: numerical
subject: community-medicine
level: third-professional-mbbs
chapter: biostatistics
topics: []
examTag: Third Professional MBBS
difficulty: medium
answer: >
  About 100 people, equivalent to a prevalence of 2% of the population.
explanation: >
  Under steady-state conditions prevalence is approximately incidence
  multiplied by average duration. Incidence is 25 new cases per 5,000
  people per year, which is 0.005 per year (0.5%), and multiplying by
  the 4-year duration gives 0.02, or 2%. Applying that proportion to the
  population of 5,000 yields about 100 currently affected people. The
  relation also explains why chronic, non-fatal disease raises prevalence
  even when incidence is unchanged.
---

## Model answer

**Step 1 — incidence.** New cases per year ÷ population at risk gives
25 ÷ 5,000 = 0.005 per year, that is, 0.5% of the population acquires
the disease each year.

**Step 2 — apply the steady-state relation.** Prevalence ≈ incidence ×
average duration, so 0.005 × 4 years = 0.02, which is a prevalence of
2%.

**Step 3 — convert to a count.** Two per cent of 5,000 people is
100 people, so about one hundred individuals have the disease at any
given moment.

In words: each year's 25 new cases persist for about 4 years, so roughly
four annual "batches" of 25 patients — around 100 people — coexist at
any point. Revise the underlying concepts on the
[incidence vs prevalence note](/notes/incidence-vs-prevalence/) and the
[community medicine quiz page](/practice/quiz/community-medicine/).
