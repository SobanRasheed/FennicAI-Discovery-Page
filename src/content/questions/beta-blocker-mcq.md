---
question: >
  Propranolol lowers blood pressure in a patient with hypertension mainly
  through which mechanism?
type: mcq
subject: pharmacology
level: second-professional-mbbs
chapter: cardiovascular-drugs
topics: []
examTag: Second Professional MBBS
difficulty: easy
options:
  - Blockade of beta-1 receptors, reducing heart rate, contractility and renin release
  - Blockade of alpha-1 receptors, dilating resistance vessels
  - Stimulation of beta-2 receptors, relaxing vascular smooth muscle
  - Blockade of voltage-gated calcium channels in arteriolar smooth muscle
answer: Blockade of beta-1 receptors, reducing heart rate, contractility and renin release
explanation: >
  Propranolol is a non-selective beta blocker; its antihypertensive
  effect comes from three beta-1 consequences — lower heart rate, lower
  contractility (so lower cardiac output) and reduced renin secretion from
  juxtaglomerular cells, which together lower both output and angiotensin
  II-mediated tone. The key distractor, alpha-1 blockade, is the mechanism
  of prazosin, while calcium channel blockade belongs to amlodipine; the
  beta-2 option is not a beta blocker action at all.
---

## Question

Remember the two exam warnings attached to propranolol: it is
non-selective, so beta-2 blockade can precipitate bronchospasm in
asthmatics, and it is contraindicated in heart block and severe
bradycardia. Cardioselective agents such as atenolol or metoprolol are
preferred when bronchial risk exists. For the receptor theory behind
this, revise the [autonomic pharmacology note](/notes/autonomic-pharmacology/)
and the [pharmacology quiz page](/practice/quiz/pharmacology/).
