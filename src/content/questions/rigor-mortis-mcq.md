answer: The small muscles of the eyelids, face and jaw, spreading downward to the limbs
explanation: >
  Rigor follows Nysten's law: it begins in the small muscles of the
  eyelids, face and jaw, then descends through the neck, trunk and arms
  to the legs, and passes off in the same order. Onset is hastened by
  heat, exercise before death and low muscle mass, and delayed by cold.
  The key distractor, beginning in the large limb muscles, is the common
  misconception; cardiac muscle is the first muscle of the body to
  become rigid but is not part of the visible skeletal sequence.

## Question

Rigor mortis is one of the three classical signs of death alongside
algor mortis and livor mortis, and its timeline — full rigidity at about
12 hours in temperate conditions, passing off by 24 to 36 hours — makes
it a rough clock for time since death. Its forensic caveat: rigor fixes
the body in the posture of death, so a posture inconsistent with the
scene means the body was moved.
