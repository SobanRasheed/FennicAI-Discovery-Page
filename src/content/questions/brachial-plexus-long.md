  Describe the formation, cords, and branches of the brachial plexus, and

answer: >
  The brachial plexus is formed by the anterior rami of C5, C6, C7, C8 and
  T1, which unite into superior, middle and inferior trunks; each trunk
  divides into anterior and posterior divisions, which recombine into
  lateral, posterior and medial cords named for their position around the
  axillary artery. Erb's palsy (C5–C6) causes adduction, internal rotation
  and pronation with wrist flexion; Klumpke's palsy (C8–T1) causes a claw
  hand, sometimes with Horner syndrome.
explanation: >
  Marks are earned for the root–trunk–division–cord–branch sequence, the
  branches of each cord around the axillary artery, and the two injury
  patterns tied to the correct root levels and mechanisms of traction.

## Model answer

The plexus is built from the anterior rami of C5–T1, with a frequent
contribution from C4 (prefixation) or T2 (postfixation). Roots C5–C6 form
the superior trunk, C7 continues as the middle trunk, and C8–T1 form the
inferior trunk. Each trunk splits into anterior and posterior divisions;
the posterior divisions of all three trunks form the posterior cord, the
anterior divisions of the superior and middle trunks form the lateral
cord, and the anterior division of the inferior trunk alone continues as
the medial cord.

| Element | Key branches |
|---|---|
| Roots | Long thoracic (C5–C7), dorsal scapular (C5) |
| Superior trunk | Suprascapular, nerve to subclavius |
| Lateral cord | Lateral pectoral, musculocutaneous, lateral root of median nerve |
| Posterior cord | Upper and lower subscapular, thoracodorsal, axillary, radial |
| Medial cord | Medial pectoral, medial cutaneous nerves of arm and forearm, ulnar, medial root of median nerve |

**Erb's palsy** is an upper trunk (C5–C6) injury from downward traction of
the head away from the shoulder, classically in shoulder dystocia. Loss of
the abductors and lateral rotators of the shoulder (suprascapular and
axillary nerves) and of elbow flexors and supinators (musculocutaneous
nerve) leaves the limb adducted, internally rotated, pronated and
wrist-flexed — the "waiter's tip" — with an absent biceps jerk.

**Klumpke's palsy** is a lower trunk (C8–T1) injury from forced upward
traction, such as catching hold while falling. Paralysis of the intrinsic
hand muscles and the long digital flexors produces a claw hand; if T1
preganglionic sympathetic fibres are involved, an ipsilateral Horner
syndrome (ptosis, miosis, anhidrosis, enophthalmos) is added. Revise the
full layout on the [brachial plexus note](/notes/brachial-plexus/) and the
[anatomy quiz page](/practice/quiz/anatomy/).
