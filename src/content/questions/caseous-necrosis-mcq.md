answer: Caseous necrosis, appearing as amorphous cheese-like debris with total loss of architecture
explanation: >
  Caseous necrosis is enclosed within a granuloma and looks grossly soft,
  white and friable, like dry cheese; microscopically the tissue pattern
  is completely obliterated into amorphous granular debris, and it is
  virtually diagnostic of tuberculosis. The key distractor, fibrinoid
  necrosis, is also amorphous and eosinophilic, but it affects blood
  vessel walls in immune-mediated injury such as malignant hypertension
  or polyarteritis nodosa, not lobes of tissue framed by epithelioid
  macrophages.
