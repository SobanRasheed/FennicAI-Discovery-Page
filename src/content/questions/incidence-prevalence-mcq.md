question: >
  A district health office counts every patient newly diagnosed with
  pulmonary tuberculosis during 2025 and divides that number by the
  mid-year population at risk. Which measure has been calculated?
type: mcq
subject: community-medicine
level: third-professional-mbbs
chapter: epidemiology
topics: []
examTag: Third Professional MBBS
difficulty: easy
options:
  - The incidence rate of tuberculosis
  - The point prevalence of tuberculosis
  - The case fatality rate of tuberculosis
  - The attack rate of tuberculosis in the whole population
answer: The incidence rate of tuberculosis
explanation: >
  Incidence measures new cases arising in a population at risk over a
  defined period — exactly what the count of 2025 diagnoses divided by
  the mid-year population gives. The key distractor, prevalence, would
  include everyone already living with the disease at the start as well
  as the new cases, since prevalence is a snapshot of all existing cases.
  Case fatality divides deaths by cases, and attack rate is a
  short-incidence measure restricted to an exposed epidemic population,
  such as guests at a wedding.

## Question

The incidence-versus-prevalence distinction is the most examined idea in
basic epidemiology: incidence quantifies risk and causes, prevalence
quantifies burden and reflects incidence multiplied by duration. Chronic
disease with long survival has high prevalence at low incidence; a
rapidly fatal disease does the opposite. Revise the full comparison on
the [incidence vs prevalence note](/notes/incidence-vs-prevalence/) and
the [community medicine quiz page](/practice/quiz/community-medicine/).
