question: >
  Measles has a basic reproduction number (R0) of roughly 12 to 18.
  Approximately what proportion of a population must be immune to
  interrupt sustained measles transmission?
type: mcq

options:
  - About 92–95%, derived from the herd-immunity threshold 1 − 1/R0
  - About 60–70%, the threshold needed for infections with an R0 near 3
  - About 75%, because R0 falls once half the population is vaccinated
  - 100%, because a single susceptible person allows an outbreak
answer: About 92–95%, derived from the herd-immunity threshold 1 − 1/R0
explanation: >
  The herd-immunity threshold is 1 − 1/R0; with R0 of 12 to 18 this
  gives 1 − 1/12 = 0.917 up to 1 − 1/18 = 0.944, so roughly 92–95% of
  people must be immune. The key distractor of 60–70% is the threshold
  for a far less transmissible agent (R0 near 3), and 100% is wrong
  because the very concept of herd immunity is that immune bystanders
  break the chain of transmission and protect the susceptible remainder.

## Question

This calculation is why measles is the canary of immunisation
programmes: a two-dose schedule with even modest drop-off in coverage
dips below the threshold, which is exactly what drives school outbreaks
in under-vaccinated communities. Note R0 assumes a fully susceptible
population with typical mixing, so the effective reproduction number R
falls as immunity spreads — the threshold formula simply solves for
when R reaches 1.
