  Which feature best distinguishes apoptosis from necrosis?

  - It is an active, energy-dependent process that typically provokes little or no inflammation
  - It always follows severe ischaemia or membrane injury by toxins
  - It causes cells to swell and rupture, releasing enzymes into surrounding tissue
  - It affects large contiguous groups of cells simultaneously
answer: It is an active, energy-dependent process that typically provokes little or no inflammation
explanation: >
  Apoptosis is programmed, gene-directed cell suicide that requires ATP:
  the cell shrinks, the chromatin condenses and marginates, the membrane
  blebs, and fragments are phagocytosed without spilling contents, so no
  inflammatory response follows. The key distractor, swelling and rupture
  with enzyme release, is the exact description of necrosis, which is a
  passive consequence of ATP depletion and membrane damage and is
  accompanied by acute inflammation.

## Question

Keep the ATP dependence clinically useful: ischaemic cells with partial
injury may still apoptosis rather than necrose, and caspase activation is
the enzymatic engine of the process. Also note apoptosis is physiological
in embryonic remodelling and thymic selection, whereas necrosis is always
pathological. Full detail is on the [cell injury note](/notes/cell-injury/).
