  A newborn delivered after severe shoulder dystocia holds the affected arm
  adducted and internally rotated against the trunk, with the forearm
  pronated and the wrist flexed — the classic "waiter's tip" posture.
  Which part of the brachial plexus is injured?

options:
  - The C5 and C6 nerve roots (upper trunk), damaged by excessive downward traction on the shoulder
  - The C8 and T1 nerve roots (lower trunk), damaged by forced upward abduction of the arm
  - The C7 nerve root alone, damaged by a fall on the outstretched hand
  - The posterior cord, compressed against the humerus in the axilla
answer: The C5 and C6 nerve roots (upper trunk), damaged by excessive downward traction on the shoulder
explanation: >
  Erb's palsy injures the C5 and C6 roots forming the upper trunk, the
  classic birth injury of shoulder dystocia. Loss of the shoulder abductors
  and lateral rotators (suprascapular and axillary nerves) plus the forearm
  flexors and supinators (musculocutaneous nerve) produces the adducted,
  internally rotated, pronated "waiter's tip" posture, with an absent
  biceps jerk. The key distractor, C8 and T1, describes Klumpke's palsy,
  which instead produces a claw hand from loss of intrinsic hand muscles
  and may add Horner syndrome.

## Question

Erb's palsy is the standard first-professional exam vignette linking root
level to a visible posture, so learn the posture from the lost movements:
no abduction or lateral rotation at the shoulder, no flexion or supination
at the elbow. Downward traction of the head away from the shoulder during
a difficult delivery stretches C5–C6; forced upward traction stretches
C8–T1. For the full plexus layout, see the
[brachial plexus study note](/notes/brachial-plexus/), and test yourself
further on the [anatomy quiz page](/practice/quiz/anatomy/).
