  How many net ATP are produced when one molecule of glucose is converted
  to two molecules of lactate through anaerobic glycolysis?
type: mcq
subject: biochemistry

options:
  - 2 ATP
  - 4 ATP
  - 6 ATP
  - 30 ATP
answer: 2 ATP
explanation: >
  Glycolysis generates 4 ATP by substrate-level phosphorylation — two at
  the phosphoglycerate kinase step and two at the pyruvate kinase step —
  but 2 ATP were already spent at hexokinase and phosphofructokinase-1,
  leaving a net gain of 2 ATP per glucose. The key distractor, 4 ATP, is
  the gross production, which forgets the investment phase; roughly 30–32
  ATP belongs to complete aerobic oxidation, not to the anaerobic pathway
  alone.

## Question

The investment-then-payoff structure is the whole point of this question:
glycolysis spends ATP early to trap and activate glucose, then recovers
twice as many ATP late. This net of 2 ATP is why hypoxic tissues such as
exercising muscle or red cells (which lack mitochondria) can survive but
are metabolically expensive to run. Revise the full pathway on the
[glycolysis note](/notes/glycolysis/) and the
[biochemistry quiz page](/practice/quiz/biochemistry/).
