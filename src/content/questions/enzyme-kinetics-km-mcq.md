  In Michaelis–Menten kinetics, the Michaelis constant (Km) is best
  defined as which of the following?

options:
  - The substrate concentration at which the reaction velocity is half of Vmax
  - The reaction velocity reached when the enzyme is half-saturated
  - The maximum number of substrate molecules converted per enzyme molecule per second
  - The enzyme concentration at which the reaction becomes saturated
answer: The substrate concentration at which the reaction velocity is half of Vmax
explanation: >
  Km has the units of concentration, not velocity, and equals the
  substrate level that drives the enzyme at half its maximal rate; it is
  inversely related to apparent substrate affinity, so a low Km means high
  affinity. The key distractor, the velocity at half of Vmax, is a number
  in velocity units and cannot be a dissociation-like constant at all,
  while the turnover description defines kcat, not Km.

## Question

Km questions usually pivot on two clinical applications: comparing
isoenzymes, such as the much higher Km of glucokinase than hexokinase
(making glucokinase a glucose sensor in the liver), and interpreting
competitive inhibition, which raises the apparent Km without changing
Vmax. Always pair Km with Vmax in your reasoning, since drugs and
disease tend to move one or the other, not both.
