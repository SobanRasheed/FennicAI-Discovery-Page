options:
  - Irreversibly binding the 30S ribosomal subunit and causing misreading of mRNA during translation
  - Binding the 50S subunit and blocking the peptidyl transferase step of elongation
  - Inhibiting DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV
  - Blocking peptidoglycan cross-linking in the bacterial cell wall
answer: Irreversibly binding the 30S ribosomal subunit and causing misreading of mRNA during translation
explanation: >
  Aminoglycosides bind the 30S subunit, distort the decoding site and
  impair proofreading, so aberrant proteins are inserted into the cell
  membrane — the defective membrane itself contributes to the lethal
  effect, making these drugs bactericidal. The key distractor, 50S
  binding, is the mechanism of chloramphenicol, clindamycin and the
  macrolides, while DNA gyrase inhibition belongs to the fluoroquinolones
  and cell-wall cross-linking to the beta-lactams.

## Question

Two properties follow directly from this mechanism and are favourite
follow-up marks: transport across the bacterial inner membrane is
oxygen-dependent, so aminoglycosides are inactive against anaerobes, and
the lethal effect is enhanced by cell-wall agents such as penicillin,
giving synergy used in enterococcal endocarditis. Monitor therapy with
peak and trough levels — ototoxicity and nephrotoxicity are
concentration-related.
