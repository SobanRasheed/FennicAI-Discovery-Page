question: >
  By what mechanism does HIV enter its principal target cell, the CD4+
  helper T lymphocyte?
type: mcq
subject: microbiology
level: second-professional-mbbs
chapter: virology
topics: []
examTag: Second Professional MBBS
difficulty: hard
options:
  - The gp120 envelope glycoprotein binds CD4 and a chemokine co-receptor (CCR5 or CXCR4), triggering gp41-mediated membrane fusion
  - The gp120 glycoprotein binds CD8 on cytotoxic T cells, followed by receptor-mediated endocytosis
  - The virus is phagocytosed through complement receptors on macrophages
  - The virus fuses with the cell through integrin receptors on the T-cell surface
answer: The gp120 envelope glycoprotein binds CD4 and a chemokine co-receptor (CCR5 or CXCR4), triggering gp41-mediated membrane fusion
explanation: >
  gp120 first attaches to CD4; this uncovers a second binding site that
  must engage the chemokine co-receptor, and the conformational change
  then springs gp41, whose fusion peptide pulls the viral and cellular
  membranes together. The co-receptor defines tropism — CCR5 on
  macrophages and memory T cells in transmission, CXCR4 on activated
  T cells later in disease. The key distractor, CD8 binding, names the
  wrong receptor entirely, and co-receptor use is precisely why people
  homozygous for the CCR5-delta32 deletion resist infection.

## Question

This entry pathway is tested because it maps onto therapy: maraviroc
blocks CCR5, enfuvirtide jams gp41, and both work only before reverse
transcription, unlike the nucleoside analogues and protease inhibitors.
Also remember HIV is an enveloped, positive-sense ssRNA retrovirus
carrying its own reverse transcriptase and integrase. Test yourself
further on the [microbiology quiz page](/practice/quiz/microbiology/).
