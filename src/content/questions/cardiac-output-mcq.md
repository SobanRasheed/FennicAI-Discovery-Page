  A resting healthy adult has a stroke volume of 70 mL and a heart rate of
  72 beats per minute. What is this person's cardiac output?

  - About 500 millilitres per minute
answer: About 5 litres per minute
explanation: >
  Cardiac output is the product of stroke volume and heart rate, so
  0.07 L × 72 beats/min gives about 5.0 L/min, the normal resting value
  that also matches the volume of the circulating blood turned over once
  each minute. A distractor of about 10 litres usually comes from doubling
  the stroke volume or forgetting that the two ventricles eject in series,
  not in parallel; 0.5 litres is roughly one stroke volume per second and
  reflects mixing up per-minute and per-beat quantities.

## Question

This calculation is the foundation for every subsequent cardiac
physiology topic: the Fick principle, ejection fraction, and how
sympathetic stimulation raises output by increasing both heart rate and
contractility. Note that trained athletes reach the same 5 L/min at rest
but can raise stroke volume far more during exercise. For the underlying
events, revise the [cardiac cycle note](/notes/cardiac-cycle/) and test
yourself on the [physiology quiz page](/practice/quiz/physiology/).
