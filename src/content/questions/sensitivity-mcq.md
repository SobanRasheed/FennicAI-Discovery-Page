question: >
  A screening test for a disease has 100% sensitivity but poor
  specificity. What can be concluded when the test result is negative?
type: mcq
subject: community-medicine
level: third-professional-mbbs
chapter: biostatistics
topics:
  - diagnostic-test-accuracy
examTag: Third Professional MBBS
difficulty: medium
options:
  - The disease is effectively ruled out, because a highly sensitive test has very few false negatives
  - The disease is confirmed, because sensitivity measures the true positive rate
  - Nothing can be concluded, because only specificity determines the meaning of a negative result
  - The conclusion depends mainly on the prevalence of the disease in the screened population
answer: The disease is effectively ruled out, because a highly sensitive test has very few false negatives
explanation: >
  Sensitivity is the proportion of true diseased people who test
  positive, TP/(TP+FN); at 100% sensitivity there are no false negatives,
  so a negative result cannot belong to a diseased person — the rule
  taught as SnNout, a sensitive test with a Negative result rules the
  disease out. The key distractor reverses the roles: specificity
  governs the positive result (SpPin), and prevalence governs the
  predictive values of a positive test, not the sensitivity itself.
