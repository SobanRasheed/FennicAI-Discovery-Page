  - Inhibition of the transpeptidase enzymes (penicillin-binding proteins) that cross-link peptidoglycan strands
  - Irreversible binding to the 30S ribosomal subunit with misreading of mRNA

  - Chelation of magnesium ions required for bacterial DNA replication
answer: Inhibition of the transpeptidase enzymes (penicillin-binding proteins) that cross-link peptidoglycan strands
explanation: >
  The beta-lactam ring is a structural mimic of the terminal D-alanine–
  D-alanine of the pentapeptide side chain; transpeptidases accept it,
  are acylated, and can no longer form the peptide bridges that give the
  wall its tensile strength. The weakened wall ruptures under internal
  osmotic pressure, so penicillin is bactericidal — but only in actively
  growing cells, since inert bacteria are not building walls. The key
  distractor, 30S binding, is the aminoglycoside mechanism, not the
  beta-lactam one.

## Question

The same mimicry explains resistance: beta-lactamases open the
beta-lactam ring before it reaches its target, altered PBPs lower
affinity (as in MRSA), and gram-negatives shield their PBPs behind the
outer membrane, which is why penicillin G is a gram-positive drug. This
mechanism is the bridge topic between microbiology and pharmacology —
see the [gram-positive vs gram-negative note](/notes/gram-positive-vs-gram-negative/).
