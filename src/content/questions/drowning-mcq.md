  At autopsy, which single finding is most indicative that death was due
  to drowning?

options:
  - Abundant fine, white, often blood-tinged froth exuding from the mouth and nostrils
  - Water present in the stomach
  - Cutis anserina (goose-flesh) over the body surface
  - Hypostasis distributed over the head, neck and upper chest
answer: Abundant fine, white, often blood-tinged froth exuding from the mouth and nostrils
explanation: >
  The froth is a mixture of water, mucus, surfactant and protein-rich
  oedema fluid churned into foam by the victim's struggling respirations;
  it is copious, fine and increases on pressure, and it indicates that
  the person was breathing while submerged. The key distractor, water in
  the stomach, is unreliable — it may be swallowed agonal fluid, water
  swallowed before immersion, or contaminant entering after death.

## Question

The supporting signs have the same weakness in differing degrees: cutis
anserina reflects cold water on any living or recently dead skin,
cadaveric spasm gripping weeds or gravel in the hands is strong when
present but rare, and subpleural Paltauf haemorrhages are
non-specific. Remember the positive diagnosis of drowning is always a
diagnosis of the whole scene — no single autopsy finding settles it.
