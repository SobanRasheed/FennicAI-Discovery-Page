  In a typical case of suicidal hanging, how does the ligature mark

options:
  - An inverted V-shaped mark placed high on the neck above the thyroid cartilage, with its apex pointing to the suspension point
  - A horizontal mark at or below the level of the thyroid cartilage, uniform in depth around the whole neck
  - Multiple parallel marks crossing each other over the front of the neck
  - A faint red line confined to one side of the neck with no gap and no bruising beneath
answer: An inverted V-shaped mark placed high on the neck above the thyroid cartilage, with its apex pointing to the suspension point
explanation: >
  In hanging the weight of the body pulls the noose upward toward the
  point of suspension, so the mark is high, oblique and inverted-V
  shaped, deepest opposite the knot and interrupted where the noose
  crosses itself or the jaw lifts it off the skin. The key distractor, a
  horizontal mark at or below the thyroid cartilage, is the classic
  ligature mark of strangulation by the hands of another, where
  compressive force is horizontal and usually accompanied by bruising and
  struggling signs because the victim resists.

## Question

Never rely on the mark alone — it is a physical imprint, and a body can
be strangled and then suspended to mimic hanging. Corroborating features
of hanging include dribbling of saliva from the mouth corner, protrusion
of the tongue, and congestion above the level of the ligature. Revise
the whole asphyxia spectrum on the [asphyxia note](/notes/asphyxia/)
and the [forensic medicine quiz page](/practice/quiz/forensic-medicine/).
