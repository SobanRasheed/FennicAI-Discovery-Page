  - Plasma colloid osmotic (oncotic) pressure, generated mainly by albumin

answer: Plasma colloid osmotic (oncotic) pressure, generated mainly by albumin
explanation: >
  Along the capillary, hydrostatic pressure falls from roughly 30 to about
  15 mmHg while plasma oncotic pressure stays near 28 mmHg, so at the
  venous end the inward oncotic force dominates and fluid is reabsorbed.
  The key distractor, capillary hydrostatic pressure, acts in the opposite
  direction — it pushes fluid out of the vessel, which is why raised
  venous pressure causes oedema rather than reabsorption. Albumin matters
  most because it is abundant and too large to filter freely, so it
  sustains the osmotic gradient.

## Question

This balance explains familiar clinical facts: hypoalbuminaemia in
nephrotic syndrome or liver failure causes generalised oedema, and venous
obstruction raises capillary hydrostatic pressure to cause localised
oedema. Note that in health slightly more fluid is filtered than
reabsorbed, and the surplus returns via lymphatics — that is how the
interstitial compartment stays dry.
