question: >
  Which cranial nerve is the only one that emerges from the dorsal
  (posterior) aspect of the brainstem?
type: mcq
subject: anatomy

examTag: First Professional MBBS
difficulty: hard
options:
  - The oculomotor nerve (CN III), emerging between the cerebral peduncles
  - The trochlear nerve (CN IV), emerging just caudal to the inferior colliculus
  - The trigeminal nerve (CN V), emerging from the lateral pons at the middle cerebellar peduncle
  - The abducens nerve (CN VI), emerging at the junction of the pons and medulla
answer: The trochlear nerve (CN IV), emerging just caudal to the inferior colliculus
explanation: >
  The trochlear nerve is uniquely dorsal: it leaves the brainstem below
  the inferior colliculus, wraps around the midbrain, and crosses
  completely before exiting, so it is also the only cranial nerve to
  decussate and the only one to supply the contralateral superior oblique
  from its nucleus. The key distractor, CN VI, looks tempting because it
  shares the eye-movement group, but it emerges ventrally at the
  pontomedullary junction, as CN III does between the cerebral peduncles.
---

## Question

Two facts make CN IV a recurring exam stem: dorsal emergence and complete
decussation of its fibres. Clinically, a nuclear trochlear lesion on one
side paralyses the contralateral superior oblique, giving vertical
diplopia worst on descending stairs. Also remember it has the longest
intracranial course of any cranial nerve relative to its size, which is
why it is often injured in head trauma.
