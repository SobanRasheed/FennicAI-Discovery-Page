question: >
  A 55-year-old smoker undergoes spirometry. The FEV1 is 62% of predicted,
  the FVC is 58% of predicted, and the FEV1/FVC ratio is 0.95. Which
  pattern of lung disease do these results best fit?
type: mcq
subject: physiology
level: first-professional-mbbs
chapter: respiratory
topics: []
examTag: First Professional MBBS
difficulty: hard
options:
  - Restrictive disease, with both volumes reduced but the ratio preserved or increased
  - Obstructive disease, with air trapping lowering the FEV1/FVC ratio
  - Mixed obstructive and restrictive disease
  - Normal spirometry with an isolated reduction of the FVC
answer: Restrictive disease, with both volumes reduced but the ratio preserved or increased
explanation: >
  The defining datum is the FEV1/FVC ratio of 0.95, which is normal to
  high. Obstruction is defined by a reduced ratio (below about 0.7),
  because narrowed airways slow expiratory flow out of proportion to the
  volume exhaled; here FEV1 and FVC have fallen roughly in proportion,
  which is the signature of stiff, restricted lungs such as pulmonary
  fibrosis. The key distractor, obstructive disease, is excluded precisely
  because the ratio is preserved — in pure obstruction the FVC is
  relatively maintained while the FEV1 falls disproportionately.

## Question

The exam skill here is to read the ratio before the percentages: reduced
FEV1 with a normal or high ratio is restriction until proven otherwise,
and a supranormal ratio with small volumes should trigger a search for
interstitial lung disease or chest-wall restriction. Also expect the DLCO
to be reduced in parenchymal restriction, which helps separate it from
neuromuscular causes where DLCO is near normal.
