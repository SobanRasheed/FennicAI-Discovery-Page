answer: Femoral nerve, then femoral artery, then femoral vein, then femoral canal
explanation: >
  The sequence from lateral to medial is nerve, artery, vein, canal —
  remembered as NAVY or NAVC. The femoral nerve lies most laterally on
  iliacus, outside the femoral sheath; the artery and vein share the sheath,
  with the vein medial to the artery; and the femoral canal is the most
  medial compartment, containing fat and lymphatics. The key distractor
  reverses nerve and vessel positions, which would place the nerve inside
  the sheath next to the canal — anatomically impossible because the nerve
  never enters the femoral sheath.

## Question

The femoral canal is the exam favourite in this arrangement because its
upper opening, the femoral ring, is the site of femoral hernias, which
appear below and lateral to the pubic tubercle and strangulate often
because the ring is small and rigid. A useful practical application:
the femoral artery lies at the mid-inguinal point, and the femoral vein,
medial to it, is the standard route for central venous access in the groin.
