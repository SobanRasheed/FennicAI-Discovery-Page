    answer: >
      Learn drugs in classes, not individually: one prototype drug, its

      test the mechanism and the class effect, so a well-learned prototype
      answers questions about drugs you have never specifically memorised.

    answer: >
      Pair every mechanism with a clinical use and one key adverse effect —

      isolation; it describes a patient and expects you to reason to the

      before starting each Pharmacology chapter is the single biggest
      time-saver in the subject.

## How this subject is structured

The chapters run from principles to systems. **General Principles** covers
pharmacokinetics (what the body does to the drug) and pharmacodynamics (what
the drug does to the body) — the vocabulary for everything after.
**Autonomic Pharmacology** maps the cholinergic and adrenergic receptor
systems and their drugs, and explains a large share of the side effects you
will see clinically. **Cardiovascular Drugs** covers antihypertensives,
antiarrhythmics, heart failure therapy, and antithrombotics.
**Antimicrobial Agents** is the largest chapter for most students: the
antibacterial classes by mechanism, then antivirals, antifungals, and
antiparasitics. **CNS Drugs** finishes with anaesthetics, antiepileptics,
antidepressants, antipsychotics, and opioids.

## How to study it

Build your knowledge around mechanisms and prototypes. For each drug class,
learn one prototype thoroughly — its mechanism, its main use, and its
characteristic adverse effects — then note only where the alternatives
differ. This turns thousands of drug names into a few dozen frameworks. Our
[autonomic pharmacology study note](/notes/autonomic-pharmacology/) shows
the approach on the subject's most exam-heavy chapter, and the
[Pain pathways topic page](/topics/pain-pathways/) connects analgesia to its
underlying physiology.

Revise pharmacokinetics as numbers with meaning: half-life, bioavailability,
and therapeutic index reappear in nearly every paper, usually inside a
dosage calculation or a poisoning scenario.

Above all, test yourself with applied questions. Work through
[the Pharmacology quiz](/practice/quiz/pharmacology/) after each chapter, and
when you choose a wrong option, trace exactly which link in the reasoning
chain failed — that gap, not the fact, is what to fix.
