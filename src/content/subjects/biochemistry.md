## How this subject is structured

The chapters build in a deliberate order. **Basic Biomolecules** covers the
amino acids, sugars, fatty acids, and nucleotides that everything else is
made of. **Enzymes** explains how reactions are catalysed and regulated, and
how drugs and toxins interfere with them. **Carbohydrate Metabolism** follows
glucose through glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, glycogen storage, and
gluconeogenesis, ending in the metabolic picture of diabetes.
**Lipid Metabolism** handles fatty acid breakdown and synthesis, ketone
bodies, and the lipoproteins that matter in atherosclerosis. **Molecular
Biology** completes the subject with replication, transcription, translation,
and the basis of inherited disease.

## How to study it

Draw every pathway yourself, from a blank page. The act of redrawing
glycolysis or the citric acid cycle forces you to notice where ATP is spent
and produced, which steps are irreversible, and where regulation sits — the
exact details exams probe. Reading a printed pathway creates familiarity;
drawing it creates recall.

For each pathway, learn its clinical hook: the enzyme deficiency that breaks
it, the drug that blocks it, or the disease that dysregulates it. Our
[glycolysis study note](/notes/glycolysis/) shows this approach — the steps,
the regulation, and the clinical correlations kept together rather than
apart.

Finally, connect the subject to the rest of first year. Hormone action and
metabolism are the shared border with [Physiology](/subjects/physiology/),
and revising them once for both subjects roughly halves the work. Test
yourself regularly with [the Biochemistry quiz](/practice/quiz/biochemistry/)
so the detail stays sharp as the course accumulates.
