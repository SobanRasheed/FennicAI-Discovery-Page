## How this subject is structured

The chapters build from measurement to action. **Epidemiology** provides the
measurement tools: disease frequency, association, study design, screening,
and outbreak investigation. **Biostatistics** supplies the numerical methods
behind those measurements — data types, summary statistics, and the common
tests. **Communicable Diseases** applies both to the major infections,
covering transmission, surveillance, and control. **Environmental Health**
deals with water, sanitation, air, and occupational exposures. **Health
Systems & Programmes** finishes with how care is actually organised: primary
health care, the national programmes, and health management.

## How to study it

Treat Community Medicine as a quantitative subject with descriptive content
attached. The mark-earning core is small and stable — the measures of
disease frequency, the characteristics of screening tests, the study designs,
and the common statistical tests — so drill these until they are automatic.
Our [incidence versus prevalence study note](/notes/incidence-vs-prevalence/)
and the [Diagnostic Test Accuracy topic page](/topics/diagnostic-test-accuracy/)
cover the two areas where examiners concentrate their numericals.

For the descriptive chapters, study by structure rather than page order:
each communicable disease by its agent, transmission, incubation period,
control measures; each national programme by its objectives, target
population, and delivery strategy. Park's organises much of this already,
but condensing it yourself is what makes it retrievable in an exam hall.

Connect the subject to the rest of the curriculum where it genuinely links:
communicable disease control pairs with [Microbiology](/subjects/microbiology/),
and programme planning draws on the same population-level reasoning you meet
throughout the clinical years. Test yourself with
[the Community Medicine quiz](/practice/quiz/community-medicine/), and do
the biostatistics numericals with a pen and paper, not just by reading.
