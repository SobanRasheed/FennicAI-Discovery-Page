## How this subject is structured

The chapters follow the standard regional sequence used across MBBS
programmes. The **Upper Limb** covers the shoulder girdle through the hand,
and it is where most students meet the brachial plexus — the single most
examined topic in the region. The **Thorax** deals with the chest wall, lungs
and pleura, and the heart with its blood supply and the mediastinum that
contains them. **Head & Neck** is the largest region in most curricula: the
skull, face, scalp, the triangles of the neck, and the twelve cranial nerves.
**Abdomen & Pelvis** covers the abdominal wall, peritoneum, and the
gastrointestinal and genitourinary organs, ending with the perineum.
**Neuroanatomy** stands slightly apart as a systems-based chapter: the spinal
cord, brainstem, pathways, and the blood supply of the brain.

## How to study it

Study each region three ways: read the region, then see it — on a specimen,
model, atlas, or dissection — then test yourself on it. An atlas such as
Netter's is not optional for Anatomy; structure and relationship are visual
knowledge, and reading alone will not build it.

For every important structure, force yourself to state four things: what it
is, where it is (its relations), its nerve or blood supply, and one clinical
point — a common injury, a surgical landmark, or a sign it produces. This is
exactly the shape of viva questions and of most SEQ stems.

Finally, study the recurring topics deeply rather than everything thinly. The
[brachial plexus study note](/notes/brachial-plexus/) covers one of these
core topics in full, and you can drill the whole subject with
[the Anatomy quiz](/practice/quiz/anatomy/).
