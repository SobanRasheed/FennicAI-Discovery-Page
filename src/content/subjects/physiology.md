## How this subject is structured

The chapters move from the unit of life outward. **Cell Physiology** covers
membrane transport, the action potential, and cell signalling — the
vocabulary for everything that follows. The **Cardiovascular** chapter
handles cardiac electrophysiology, the cardiac cycle, cardiac output, and
blood pressure regulation. The **Respiratory** chapter covers breathing
mechanics, gas exchange and transport, and ventilatory control. The **Renal**
chapter explains filtration, tubular function, urine concentration, and
acid-base balance. The **Endocrine** chapter ties the systems together
through hormonal control.

## How to study it

Physiology is a mechanisms subject, so study it causally: for every process,
be able to answer "what happens next, and why?" Take the cardiac cycle —
learn the pressure changes, then the valve movements they cause, then the
heart sounds those produce, and you have answered a whole family of exam
questions in one chain. Our [cardiac cycle study note](/notes/cardiac-cycle/)
walks through exactly this kind of linked reasoning.

Second, study normal values actively. Questions frequently hinge on knowing
one number — a filtration rate, a ventilation volume, a plasma concentration
— and what happens when it moves. Make a one-page list of normal values and
revise it weekly rather than the night before.

Third, test yourself with vignette-style MCQs, because applied questions are
where marks are won. Work through [the Physiology quiz](/practice/quiz/physiology/)
regularly, and pair it with [the Biochemistry subject page](/subjects/biochemistry/)
where the two subjects meet on metabolism and signalling.
